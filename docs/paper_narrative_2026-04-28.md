### 1. Headline / proposed paper title (1-2 alternatives)

1. Bottleneck-Typed Retrieval Augmentation for Legal and Multi-Hop Question Answering
2. When Retrieval Depth Predicts RAG Method Choice: Snap-Conditioned Retrieval Across Legal MC and Multi-Hop QA

### 2. Abstract draft (under 250 words)

Recent legal and retrieval-augmented QA studies report that retrieval can help, fail to help, or harm depending on the benchmark, but the dominant explanation remains retriever quality rather than task bottleneck structure (docs/lit_review_2026-04-28.md). We propose a bottleneck taxonomy in which datasets are characterized by their retrieval-depth signature: on MuSiQue with Llama 70B, restricting `rag_simple` from top-5 to top-1 retrieval reduces EM from 27.5% to 13.0% (-14.5pp, paired N=200, McNemar p=4.176981747e-07), while on BarExam with Gemma 4 26B the same diagnostic is flat at 82.5% top-5 versus 83.0% top-1 (-0.5pp, p=1.00 NS; docs/signoff_log.md; docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md). This contrast motivates a retrieval-bottlenecked versus reasoning-bottlenecked account.

Snap-conditioned retrieval is the demonstration vehicle, not the paper's sole contribution. This preempts the FLARE critique: the primitive is fixed-cost and question-scoped, while the central claim is the bottleneck signature rather than a renamed iterative retrieval method (docs/lit_review_2026-04-28.md). In its two-call form, the model first produces a tentative answer and a HyDE-style passage, retrieves on that passage, and then synthesizes a final answer. On MuSiQue, `snap_hyde_2call` improves Llama 70B from 27.5% to 37.0% (+9.5pp, paired N=200, p=0.0079; docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md). On BarExam, the signed Gemma 4 26B-A4B Tier 3 result is 78.08% to 81.17% (+3.09pp, N=1195), with Gemma 4 E4B also improving from 58.49% to 62.18% (+3.69pp, N=1195; docs/signoff_log.md). The method is not universal: Gemma 27B on MuSiQue is NULL at 28.5% to 23.0% (-5.5pp, p=0.13), explained by HyDE passages lowering gold-hit from 84.0% to 76.5% (docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md).

### 3. Introduction draft (~3 paragraphs)

Retrieval-augmented generation is often treated as a monotone improvement over closed-book answering, but recent legal QA evidence contradicts that assumption. Zheng et al. report that standard retrieval gives only an approximately +0.5pp gain on BarExam while giving +23pp on HousingQA, and Vaddi reports that RAG improves ContractNLI by +5pp while hurting CaseHOLD by -8pp (docs/lit_review_2026-04-28.md). These findings are usually interpreted as evidence that legal retrievers must become better legal reasoners or that query expansion must be improved (docs/lit_review_2026-04-28.md). That interpretation is incomplete: it treats retrieval failure as a local retriever defect rather than as a property of the benchmark-model pair.

We reframe this asymmetry as a bottleneck spectrum. A dataset is retrieval-bottlenecked when increasing retrieval depth materially changes answer accuracy, and reasoning-bottlenecked when answer accuracy is comparatively insensitive to retrieval depth. The paired diagnostic is simple: compare the same method at top-1 and top-5 retrieved passages. On MuSiQue with Llama 70B, `rag_simple` falls from 27.5% to 13.0% when restricted from top-5 to top-1 (-14.5pp, paired N=200, p=4.176981747e-07; docs/signoff_log.md). On BarExam with Gemma 4 26B, the analogous `rag_simple` comparison is 82.5% at top-5 and 83.0% at top-1 (-0.5pp, p=1.00 NS; docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md). This roughly 14pp gap supplies a method-independent retrieval-depth signature for the taxonomy (CLAUDE.md).

The paper's contribution is therefore a typology plus a method demonstration. The typology predicts when retrieval shaping should matter; `snap_hyde_2call` demonstrates the claim on the retrieval-bottlenecked side by improving Llama 70B MuSiQue from 27.5% to 37.0% (+9.5pp, paired N=200, p=0.0079; docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md). The same family of snap-conditioned methods also accounts for the legal MC side, where Gemma 4 26B-A4B BarExam improves from `rag_simple` 78.08% to `rag_snap_hyde` 81.17% (+3.09pp, N=1195), and Gemma 4 E4B improves from 58.49% to 62.18% (+3.69pp, N=1195; docs/signoff_log.md). The contribution must be stated with model-conditional caveats: on MuSiQue, Gemma 27B `snap_hyde_2call` is 23.0% versus a 28.5% `rag_simple` baseline (-5.5pp, paired N=200, p=0.13), and the mechanism audit attributes the sign reversal to HyDE-passage gold-hit falling from 84.0% to 76.5% for Gemma while rising from 84.0% to 86.5% for Llama (docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md).

### 4. Related Work positioning (~2 paragraphs)

Snap-conditioned retrieval belongs in the lineage of generated-query and draft-conditioned retrieval. HyDE generates hypothetical documents for dense retrieval; Query2Doc generates pseudo-documents for query expansion; FLARE retrieves from tentative generations when low-confidence tokens appear; and Speculative RAG drafts answers after retrieval and verifies them with a larger model (docs/lit_review_2026-04-28.md). The closest prior for reviewer purposes is FLARE, but the distinction should be explicit: `snap_hyde_2call` is question-scoped rather than token- or sentence-scoped, fixed at two LLM calls rather than iterative, and unconditional rather than gated on low-confidence tokens (docs/lit_review_2026-04-28.md; docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md). The method should not be presented as if no predecessor exists; it is better positioned as a fixed-cost, answer-committing variant of the broader draft-then-retrieve family.

The stronger novelty lies in bottleneck framing. Adaptive-RAG routes individual questions by complexity, Know-Your-RAG classifies questions into types such as fact, summary, reasoning, and unanswerable, and GRADE organizes multi-hop difficulty by reasoning depth and semantic distance (docs/lit_review_2026-04-28.md). These taxonomies operate at the per-question level. Vaddi is the closest legal-RAG precedent because it reports task-dependent retrieval benefit and harm, including +5pp on ContractNLI and -8pp on CaseHOLD, but it does not provide a per-dataset bottleneck typology or a method selection rule (docs/lit_review_2026-04-28.md). The positioning claim should be narrow: this work proposes a per-dataset bottleneck typology, measured by retrieval-depth sensitivity, that predicts which retrieval-shaping primitive should be useful.

### 5. Method section skeleton

The method section should define `snap_hyde_2call` as a fixed two-call architecture. Call 1 produces both a snap answer and a HyDE-style passage; retrieval is run on the HyDE passage; call 2 synthesizes the final answer from the snap, question, and retrieved evidence (docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md). This replaces the earlier three-call `rag_snap_hyde` structure, whose MuSiQue top-5 result was 24.0%, with a two-call mode that reaches 37.0% on the same Llama 70B N=200 setting (+13pp versus three-call `rag_snap_hyde`, p=0.0001; docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md).

The first reviewer mitigation is the one-call ablation. The landed `snap_hyde_1call` variant retrieves on the bare question and then uses one LLM call for snap reasoning plus final answer; it reaches 30.5% versus 27.5% for `rag_simple` (+3.0pp, paired N=200, p=0.31 NS), while `snap_hyde_2call` reaches 37.0% (+9.5pp, paired N=200, p=0.008 SIG; docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md). The comparison between one-call and two-call variants is -6.5pp for one-call with p=0.072 (b/c=29/16), so the current evidence attributes roughly +3pp to inline snap-CoT and roughly +6.5pp to the dedicated synthesis call with HyDE-conditioned retrieval (docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md).

The second mitigation is the retrieval-depth ablation. `snap_hyde_2call` drops from 37.0% at top-5 to 24.0% at top-1 (-13pp, p=6.88e-05), with evidence length verified at one passage for 200/200 records (docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md). Across four Llama 70B MuSiQue methods, top-1 causes large drops: `rag_simple` -14.5pp (p=4.18e-07), `multi_hyde_diverse` -16.5pp (p=5.42e-07), `rag_snap_hyde` -10.0pp (p=0.0012), and `rag_multi_query` -15.0pp (p=5.30e-06; docs/top1_ablation_2026-04-28.md). This supports the claim that retrieval depth is a first-order variable on MuSiQue.

The third mitigation is the pure-HyDE comparator. On Llama 70B MuSiQue N=200, `rag_hyde` reaches 18.0%, which is -9.5pp versus `rag_simple` (p=0.005, b/c=31/12), while `snap_hyde_2call` beats `rag_hyde` by +19pp at equal two-call budget (p=6.97e-08, b/c=45/7; docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md). Thus, the method section should state that the snap condition is not a cosmetic prefix to HyDE; in this setting it changes harmful question-only HyDE into useful answer-anchored retrieval.

### 7. Discussion / Limits

The main limit is model conditionality on retrieval-bottlenecked datasets. The Gemma 27B MuSiQue result is not merely an unexplained null: `snap_hyde_2call` is 23.0% versus 28.5% for `rag_simple` (-5.5pp, paired N=200, p=0.13), and the associated retrieval audit shows gold-hit falling from 84.0% to 76.5% for Gemma while rising from 84.0% to 86.5% for Llama (docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md). The paper should therefore claim a mechanism, not model agnosticism: on retrieval-bottlenecked tasks, answer-conditioned HyDE helps when it improves gold recall and harms when it moves retrieval away from gold passages.

CaseHOLD and LegalBench-SCALR also prevent a binary taxonomy. The Llama 70B CaseHOLD comparison is 72.0% for `rag_simple` and 69.5% for `snap_hyde_2call` (-2.5pp, p=0.49 NS), and LegalBench-SCALR replicates the direction at 77.0% versus 75.0% (-2.0pp, p=0.50 NS). CaseHOLD has `gold_retrieved` 0/200 because the correct option is one of the holdings already shown in the prompt rather than a separate gold passage; SCALR has meaningful gold retrieval but it is flat at 54.0% to 55.0% (docs/snap_hyde_2call_2026-04-28.md). This supports a third option-disambiguation class in which retrieval can introduce competing holdings rather than evidence.

The legal result should also avoid "oracle" language for `golden_passage`. On Gemma 4 26B-A4B BarExam N=1195, `golden_passage` is 78.66% versus 79.75% for `llm_only` (-1.09pp), with 96 previously correct answers flipped wrong and 83 previously wrong answers flipped correct (docs/golden_paradox_audit_2026-04-27.md). Across the same N=1195, `rag_snap_hyde` beats `rag_simple` by a 124-win / 87-loss split and +3.10pp, while `snap_only_in_final` accounts for most of the lift over `rag_simple` (docs/golden_paradox_audit_2026-04-27.md; docs/methods_vs_golden_audit_2026-04-27.md). The discussion should describe this as snap-anchored reasoning with marginal retrieval support, not as ordinary retrieval beating an oracle.

Serving and provider effects remain material. OpenRouter-served Gemma produced runaway-loop generations on iterative or multi-step prompts, including single queries taking 600s, 1200s, or 2400s, and the `gemma4_full` MuSiQue run was killed at q431/2400 with a partial `rag_simple` rate of 30.9% (docs/signoff_log.md). The signed recommendation is to prefer cluster vLLM for Gemma when citing Gemma results (docs/signoff_log.md). Separately, the full-corpus Llama 70B N=2400 paired replicate is blocked by Groq rate limits: the repo note records a 1,000 requests/day limit against approximately 4,800 calls needed for the full paired run (CLAUDE.md). These are methodology limits, not just operational inconveniences.
