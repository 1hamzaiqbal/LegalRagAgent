\subsection{Why \hyre{} Helps: The Query--Gold Affinity Gap}\label{subsec:mechanism}

The dataset-level pattern---\hyre{} bridges retrieval on BarExamQA and reaches
parity on HousingQA---invites a per-question explanation. We test one directly,
in the space the retriever actually uses. For every question with a labeled gold
passage we score the cross-encoder affinity $\mathrm{CE}(q,g)$ between the raw
question $q$ and its gold passage $g$, and $\mathrm{CE}(s,g)$ between the
\hyre{} search passage $s$ and the same gold. The quantity that matters is the
\emph{movement} $\Delta_{\mathrm{CE}} = \mathrm{CE}(s,g)-\mathrm{CE}(q,g)$: how
much the generated passage pulls the query toward its target in reranker space.

$\Delta_{\mathrm{CE}}$ explains \hyre{}'s retrieval gain far better than any
surface property of the question. Pooled across models and datasets it tracks
the \hyre{}-minus-raw Hit@5 movement at Spearman $0.44$ (cosine-space movement
$0.37$), versus $\le 0.07$ for question length and $\le 0.02$ for corpus
perplexity. The relationship is monotone: binning questions by
$\Delta_{\mathrm{CE}}$, the bottom quintile---where \hyre{} moves \emph{away}
from gold---loses $36.4$\pp{} of retrieval exposure and $6.4$\pp{} of answer
accuracy, while the top quintile gains $+26.9$\pp{} and $+2.6$\pp{}. \hyre{}
moves toward the gold passage on roughly $80\%$ of questions, which is why it
helps on net.

This makes the ``lexical gap'' precise: it is not missing vocabulary
(out-of-vocabulary rates are under $1\%$ on both corpora) but a \emph{phrasing}
gap between how a question is asked and how its controlling authority is
written. \hyre{} helps when its draft reconstructs that authority's framing and
hurts when it commits to the wrong one. A HousingQA question asking whether
eviction is ``first heard in justice-of-the-peace court'' shares almost no
language with the statute, which grants the ``district court\,\dots
jurisdiction over forcible entry and detainer''; \hyre{} writes that
jurisdictional framing and recovers the gold passage. In the failure case,
\hyre{} answers an Arkansas appeal-bond question by asserting there is ``no
statutory requirement,'' drifts toward appellate-procedure text, and
\emph{breaks} a retrieval the raw query had gotten right.

The answer-level correlation is weak ($<0.07$ on every axis): closing the
retrieval gap is necessary but not sufficient, and the residual
answer-conversion bottleneck---sharpest on HousingQA's binary entailment---is
the harder remaining problem.
