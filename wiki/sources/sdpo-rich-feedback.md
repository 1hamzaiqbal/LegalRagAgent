# Reinforcement Learning via Self-Distillation - SDPO

## TL;DR

Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO) samples an attempt, obtains
tokenized environment feedback or a successful peer rollout, and has the same
policy re-score the original prefixes under that feedback. The log-probability
shift supplies dense hindsight pseudo-advantages without an external teacher.
The paper reports faster and better learning than GRPO on several science,
tool-use, and coding settings, with a clear capacity dependence.

SDPO is a strong adjacent precedent for model-specific hindsight credit and a
direct collision with the broad claim that interaction context can be
compressed into weights. Its log-ratio is **not causal external-action value**:
there is no forced internal/external outcome contrast, common action crossed
over readers, action price, cross-model teacher policy, or target-student
regret.

## Method

For a student rollout `y ~ pi_theta(. | x)`, construct a self-teacher from the
same model under a feedback-augmented prompt `q_theta(. | x, f, y_<t)`. The
teacher does not generate a corrected answer for the core loss; it re-evaluates
the student's original prefixes. The token/logit pseudo-advantage is:

`A_SDPO(y_hat) = log q_theta(y_hat | x, f, y_<t) - log pi_theta(y_hat | x, y_<t)`.

This is a feedback-conditioned log-ratio, explicitly biased with respect to
expected task reward. It is motivated as a lower-variance bootstrapped signal,
not an unbiased causal advantage.

Feedback can contain:

- environment output such as runtime/unit-test errors;
- another successful rollout generated by the same student for that question;
  or
- both, with the released configuration normally using environment feedback
  when no peer success exists.

The implementation uses top-K logits plus a tail bucket to avoid storing full
teacher/student distributions. Reported K is 100 without rich feedback and 20
for rich-feedback/test-time runs. The paper uses JSD in the scalar-only setting
and reverse KL for rich-feedback and test-time experiments, with EMA or
trust-region teacher regularization.

## Main evidence

### Scalar-only science and tool-use environments

The paper evaluates Qwen3-8B and OLMo3-7B-Instruct on four SciKnowEval L3
science subsets plus ToolAlpaca. It summarizes aggregate final accuracy as
70.2% for SDPO versus 66.6% for GRPO, but SDPO wins seven of ten 5-hour
model/task cells rather than every cell.

Positive anchors include OLMo Chemistry 80.0 versus 56.7, Qwen Chemistry 80.9
versus 74.5, and Qwen Physics 75.6 versus 72.7. Negative cells include Qwen
Biology 56.8 versus 59.9, OLMo Biology 52.8 versus 55.8, and OLMo ToolAlpaca
62.1 versus 65.0. OLMo Chemistry reaches GRPO's five-hour accuracy in about 50
minutes, and average responses are reported 3.2x shorter across tasks.

The table selects the highest achieved Avg@16 within one and five hours after
hyperparameter selection. It is not an untouched, configuration-free final-
checkpoint comparison.

### Rich-feedback coding

On 131 LiveCodeBench v6 questions with Qwen3-8B, SDPO reaches 48.8 +/- 0.6 at
step 80 versus 41.2 +/- 0.8 for GRPO and reaches GRPO's final accuracy with
four times fewer generations. The Qwen3 0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, and 8B panel shows a
growing SDPO-GRPO gap with scale. In the appendix Qwen2.5 panel, SDPO loses at
1.5B, is approximately tied at 3B, and wins at 7B. A 90%-GRPO/10%-SDPO mixture
helps the weakest Qwen3 reader but slightly hurts stronger readers.

This is directly relevant to our interest in capability-relative boundaries:
the hindsight teacher is not uniformly trustworthy across scale.

### Test-time self-distillation

SDPO also specializes Qwen3-8B to one hard coding question at a time, using 16
attempts per update. The analyzed set contains 19 hard questions, nine “very
hard,” after retaining questions where at least one compared method succeeds
within the selection procedure. At 2,750 attempts, discovery is 53.2% SDPO,
41.5% best-of-k, and 35.6% multi-turn on very-hard questions; for all hard
questions it is 78.0%, 72.3%, and 68.4%.

The selection rule and tiny question counts make this a useful demonstration,
not a general discovery law. One question is first solved by SDPO at attempt
321, but its five-seed truncated mean is 1,987; 321 must not be reported as the
average.

## Ablations that matter

- Logit-level > token-level > sequence-level SDPO > GRPO in the reported
  credit-density ablation. Sequence-level SDPO still beats GRPO, separating
  some feedback value from logit density.
- Feedback-only initial teacher accuracy is 32.5 +/- 0.5; peer-solution-only is
  42.4 +/- 1.0; both are 42.5 +/- 1.2. After training, feedback plus solution
  reaches 48.3 +/- 1.4.
- Adding the original failed attempt to feedback plus solution lowers trained
  accuracy to 44.5 +/- 1.3 and entropy from 0.38 to 0.23, revealing an
  anchoring/exploration-collapse failure mode.
- Unregularized moving teacher reaches 36.1 +/- 1.6; frozen initial teacher
  48.8 +/- 0.7; trust-region 50.6 +/- 0.9; EMA 49.3 +/- 0.3.
- Off-policy SFT on self-teacher successes reaches 42.7 on LCB versus 48.8 for
  SDPO and requires twice the generations for the same step count.
- Holdout-task average is base 43.5, SDPO 42.4, GRPO 41.8, and SFT 41.4. SDPO
  mitigates forgetting; it does not eliminate it.
- Measured step overhead is 17.1% without code execution and 5.8% when code
  execution dominates wall time.

## Exact boundary for our questions

SDPO occupies:

- feedback-conditioned same-model hindsight credit;
- token/logit-level pseudo-advantages for individual generated choices;
- model-capacity dependence of self-teacher reliability;
- rich feedback as an alternative to scalar reward; and
- test-time compression of interaction history into model weights.

It does not contain:

- repeated `do(internal)` versus `do(external)` task outcomes;
- a reader-specific causal success/reward advantage `A_r(x)`;
- action cost or price-dependent net value;
- one canonical external action/payload crossed over readers;
- a strong-teacher action oracle transferred to a weaker student; or
- target regret from following that teacher oracle.

Do not call the SDPO log-ratio “action value.” For the primary
[[research-question-recommendation-2026-07-17]] study it is an auxiliary proxy
or downstream training baseline whose agreement with repeated forced-action
outcomes can be tested.

For the secondary skill study, SDPO directly closes generic “feedback/context
to weights” novelty. The narrower question survives because SDPO uses dynamic
attempt-specific feedback, not fixed versioned skill bytes; it has no
artifact-by-reader matrix or same-artifact contextual versus post-withdrawal
ordering.

## Design lessons

1. Record immediate task value separately from a self-teacher log-ratio.
2. Condition any self-distillation baseline on reader capability; include an
   outcome-derived mixture/gate for weak readers.
3. Preserve environment feedback when no successful sibling exists, but test
   anchoring from including the failed attempt.
4. Cross feedback composition, teacher regularization, and divergence rather
   than treating them as implementation details.
5. If the skill-lifecycle study reaches training, condition the self-teacher on
   the exact fixed skill, remove the skill at evaluation, and compare with
   context-only use and matched direct SFT/RL from the same checkpoint.
