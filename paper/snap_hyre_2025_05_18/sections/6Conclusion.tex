\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\hyre{} encodes a small piece of expert legal reasoning into a fixed two-call
pipeline: form an intuition about the controlling doctrine, retrieve evidence
by that frame, then judge the evidence on its own without anchoring to the
intuition. The lift over raw-question RAG tracks the question-corpus
vocabulary gap because that gap is precisely the regime where the intuition
step matters --- when the question's surface words don't yet point to the
right authority. On BarExamQA, where fact-pattern questions barely share
words with the doctrinal corpus, \hyre{} improves Hit@5 and final accuracy
across all three model sizes, matches or exceeds the labeled gold-passage
oracle on Gemma 4 26B and Llama 3.3 70B, and delivers the best correct-answer
count per million answer-stage tokens among non-oracle methods. On
HousingQA, where statutory-entailment questions already share vocabulary
with the housing statutes, \hyre{} reaches answer parity with raw-question
RAG; adding one real statute as an example in the first-call prompt then
raises Hit@5 above the raw baseline on a 500-question slice while holding
answer accuracy at parity. Pooled across all five evaluated model--dataset
configurations, \hyre{} averages +1.1\pp{} over raw-question RAG and is at
parity with HyDE.

The takeaway is practical: when the raw question is a poor retrieval handle,
let the model form an intuition before it searches, but force the final
answer back to the corpus. \hyre{} is the right tool in that regime and a
clean parity baseline when the question already reads like a passage from
the corpus.
