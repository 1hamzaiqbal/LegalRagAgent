\section{Results}\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{When Snap-HyRE Helps Answers}

\input{tables/current_answer_matrix.tex}

\hyre{} helps in proportion to the question-corpus vocabulary gap. BarExamQA
fact patterns describe accidents and transactions in narrative prose while
the support corpus uses doctrinal language; raw-question RAG retrieves the
labeled rule passage in only 1.4\% of cases at Hit@5. \hyre{} raises Hit@5
to 9.5\%, 12.1\%, and 11.0\% across the 8B, 26B, and 70B models, and
those retrieval gains convert to answer gains over raw-question RAG: 54.5\%
to 56.9\% for Llama 3.1 8B, 78.0\% to 82.0\% for Gemma 4 26B, and 74.6\% to
79.7\% for Llama 3.3 70B. Averaged across the three models, \hyre{}
reaches 72.9\%, compared with 69.0\% for raw-question RAG and 72.2\% for
HyDE.

HousingQA is the opposite end of that axis. Statutory-entailment questions
ask directly about landlord and tenant terms (``Can a landlord evict a
tenant for committing waste?''), so the question already shares vocabulary
with the target housing statutes; raw-question retrieval reaches 36.9\%
Hit@5 with no query generation. With less of a gap to close, \hyre{}
delivers answer parity rather than lift: 59.0\% / 59.6\% on 8B / 70B versus
raw-question RAG at 62.3\% / 62.1\%. Pooled across all five evaluated
model--dataset configurations, \hyre{} averages 67.4\% accuracy versus 66.3\% for
raw-question RAG (+1.1\pp{}) and 67.6\% for HyDE (parity).
\S\ref{subsec:housing_promising} shows that even the small HousingQA gap
can be closed: a single statute-style exemplar in the \hyre{} prompt
recovers Hit@5 above the raw baseline.

On HousingQA the picture is reversed because raw queries already share
vocabulary with the corpus. Raw-question retrieval reaches 36.9\% Hit@5,
and the generated-query rows trade some labeled-evidence exposure for the
broader corpus context they retrieve (still useful for the answer model, as
the parity accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:answer_matrix} shows).
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:topk} reports Hit@1/3/5/10 and MRR@10 from full
top-10 retrieval runs.

The extra evidence is useful but incomplete. \hyre{} converts a +9.5\pp{}
Hit@5 and +4.8\pp{} MRR@5 retrieval shift into +3.9\pp{} average BarExamQA
answer accuracy over raw-question RAG, while exact gold evidence still appears
in only about one in nine top-five lists. The remaining gap points to ranking,
option mapping, and evidence use.

\subsection{Snap-HyRE Matches the Gold Oracle on Larger Models}

On BarExamQA, \hyre{} matches or exceeds the labeled gold passage on the two
larger models. Gemma 4 26B reaches 82.0\% with \hyre{} versus 78.6\% with the
gold passage; Llama 3.3 70B reaches 79.7\% versus 79.2\%. Only Llama 3.1 8B
trails the oracle (56.9\% versus 60.0\%). Averaged across the three model
slices, \hyre{} reaches 72.9\% and the gold-passage oracle reaches 72.6\%. The
retrieved top-5 list does more work than the labeled rule alone: surrounding
legal passages supply context the answer model uses. The same pattern shows up
at the oracle level in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:additional}: adding
neighbors to the gold passage also improves accuracy on Llama 3.1 8B and
Gemma 4 26B.

\subsection{Gains Grow with Model Size}

\hyre{} gains over raw-question RAG grow with model size on BarExamQA: +2.4\pp{}
at 8B, +4.0\pp{} at 26B, +5.1\pp{} at 70B. Hit@5 also grows from 9.5\% (8B)
to 12.1\% (26B) and 11.0\% (70B). Bigger models both write more useful search
text and convert the retrieved evidence into more answers.

\subsection{Best Answer-Token Efficiency Among Non-Oracle Methods}

The appendix efficiency table (Table~\ref{tab:usage_metrics}) reports
correct answers per million tokens that the answer call processes. \hyre{}
answers 268.3 questions correctly per million answer-stage tokens, compared
with 244.5 for HyDE and 241.5 for raw-question RAG. The first-stage
query-generation call is logged separately and is not included in this
denominator; the lift comes from a shorter average answer context (2,377
vs 2,634 tokens for raw-question RAG) at higher accuracy. The gold-passage
control reaches 404.5 correct per million tokens because it sees only the
single labeled passage.

\subsection{Sharpening the Intuition with One Example}\label{subsec:housing_promising}

The first call's prompt can be improved with a single example. We add one
real passage from the target corpus to the \hyre{} generation prompt ---
its question, answer, and document id stripped --- and re-run the first
call. On 500-question probe slices for Gemma 4 26B
(Table~\ref{tab:exemplar_probe}), this lifts retrieval on every benchmark
we tested. The size of the lift tracks how unfamiliar the target corpus
phrasing is: small on BarExamQA (+0.6\pp{} Hit@5), where \hyre{} already
writes in doctrinal language; +3.0\pp{} on state-filtered HousingQA, where
statutes use specific terms of art; and +7.6\pp{} on Legal-Link-EU, where
EU citation forms carry the bulk of the relevance signal. Paired answer
accuracy on the HousingQA 500-question slice is 62.8\% with the example
versus 63.0\% for plain \hyre{} ($-0.20$\pp{}, $p=1.0$): the example
recovers retrieval without costing answer accuracy. The same expert-reasoning
loop runs, with the first-call intuition calibrated by one corpus example;
on an Ohio writ-of-execution question, for instance, the example-prompted
passage names ``writ of restitution'' and ``execution'' in statute style
and retrieves the gold Ohio statute at rank 3, where plain \hyre{} drifts
to neighboring writ-of-possession language.

Legal-Link-EU also marks where \hyre{}'s advantage ends. Its questions cite
source and target document anchors directly, so the raw question already
is a strong corpus search (Hit@5 90.0\% vs plain \hyre{} 68.2\%). When the
user has already framed their need as a search query, the intuition step
isn't needed. The typical legal scenario in practice is the other way
around --- a fact pattern, a tenant complaint, an audit notice that the
user has to turn into a search query before any retrieval can happen ---
and that is where \hyre{} targets its gains. The one-example variant still
helps in the boundary case: it lifts Legal-Link-EU Hit@5 from 68.2\% to
75.8\%.
