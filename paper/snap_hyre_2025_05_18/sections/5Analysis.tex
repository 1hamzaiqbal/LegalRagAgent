\section{Results}\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{When Snap-HyRE Helps Answers}

\input{tables/current_answer_matrix.tex}

BarExamQA is the clearest positive setting for \hyre{}. Raw-question RAG is a
weak retrieval handle: its Hit@5 is 1.4\%. \hyre{} raises Hit@5 to 9.5\%,
12.1\%, and 11.1\% across the 8B, 26B, and 70B model slices. Those retrieval
gains convert to final answer gains over raw-question RAG: 54.5\% to 56.9\%
for Llama 3.1 8B, 78.0\% to 82.0\% for Gemma 4 26B, and 74.6\% to 79.7\% for
Llama 3.3 70B. Averaged across the three BarExamQA model slices, \hyre{}
reaches 72.9\% accuracy, compared with 69.0\% for raw-question RAG and 72.2\%
for HyDE.

HousingQA tells a different story. The unfiltered national corpus made
retrieval mostly a wrong-jurisdiction problem; adding the state filter raises
raw Hit@5 from 2.8\% to 36.9\%. On the included state-filtered rows,
raw-question RAG is strong: 62.3\% for 8B and 62.1\% for 70B. HyDE reaches
59.1\% on the 8B row, and \hyre{} reaches 59.6\% on the 70B row. This is a
useful negative boundary: if the question already names the jurisdiction and
issue, state-filtered raw retrieval can be hard to improve.

\subsection{Did the Right Evidence Appear?}

\input{tables/current_retrieval_matrix.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval_matrix} separates retrieval from answer selection. On
BarExamQA, generated legal passages improve exact evidence exposure: raw
retrieval averages 1.4\% Hit@5, HyDE averages 10.0\%, and \hyre{} averages
10.9\%. MRR@5 moves in the same direction, from 0.7\% for raw-question
retrieval to 5.0\% for HyDE and 5.4\% for \hyre{}.

On HousingQA, the state filter is the dominant retrieval correction. Raw
state-filtered retrieval reaches 37.0\% Hit@5, while the included generated
rows retrieve less exact labeled evidence. The appendix reports Hit@1/3/5/10
and MRR@10 from full top-10 retrieval runs.

The extra evidence is useful but incomplete. \hyre{} converts a +9.5\pp{}
Hit@5 and +4.7\pp{} MRR@5 retrieval shift into +3.9\pp{} average BarExamQA
answer accuracy over raw-question RAG, while exact gold evidence still appears
in only about one in nine top-five lists. The remaining gap points to ranking,
option mapping, and evidence use.

\subsection{How the Win Happens}

The case study shows the intended design pattern. The raw query is dominated by
surface accident facts. HyDE moves toward legal language but remains broad.
\hyre{} first forms the private draft judgment that the later fall is foreseeable
because the original injury required crutches; retrieval from that frame
surfaces compact proximate-cause rule passages. The final answerer still sees
only the original question and retrieved legal text, so the win is a
retrieval-query win, not answer leakage. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:examples}
lists the compact worked examples used to sanity-check this mechanism.

\subsection{Cost and Context}

\hyre{} and HyDE both add a first call before retrieval, so their end-to-end
footprint is two logical model calls. The answer-stage token logs ask a
narrower question: after retrieval, how much text does the scored answer call
process? On rows with token logs, \hyre{} averages 1,762 answer-stage tokens
per question, compared with 2,634 for raw-question RAG and 2,442 for HyDE. The
query-generation call is separate and reported in Appendix~\ref{tab:usage_metrics}.

\subsection{Evidence Is Not the Only Bottleneck}

Oracle rows show what remains after retrieval is removed. On BarExamQA, gold
passage accuracy is only modestly above the best generated-query rows, so
option mapping and evidence use still bind. On HousingQA, gold passages remain
far above LLM-only, showing headroom when the correct statute is supplied.
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:additional} reports the oracle and rewrite controls.
