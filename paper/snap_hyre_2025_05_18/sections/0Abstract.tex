\begin{abstract}
Legal RAG often fails before the answer model sees useful law: the question
and the corpus speak different languages. A bar-exam question describes
``a driver and a banana peel''; the supporting passage states a rule about
proximate cause. HyDE addresses this by letting the model write a synthetic
passage in the corpus's voice and searching with that text instead of the
original question \citep{gao2023hyde}. We introduce \hyre{}, a two-call
extension that mirrors expert legal reasoning. The first call writes a
private draft answer (an intuition about the controlling doctrine) and,
separately, a legal search passage; only the passage is used for retrieval,
and the draft is withheld from the second call so the final answer is judged
against the corpus, not the guess. On BarExamQA, \hyre{} improves
raw-question RAG by up to 5.1 percentage points across three model sizes,
raises top-five evidence retrieval from 1.4\% to 9.5--12.1\%, and matches or
exceeds the labeled gold-passage oracle on Gemma 4 26B and Llama 3.3 70B. On
HousingQA, where the question already shares vocabulary with the target
statute, \hyre{} holds answer parity with raw-question RAG, and a single
in-prompt statute example then lifts retrieval above the raw baseline.
Pooled across five model--dataset configurations, \hyre{} averages +1.1\pp{}
over raw-question RAG and is at parity with HyDE. The pattern is clean:
\hyre{} pays off in proportion to the question--corpus vocabulary gap.
\end{abstract}
