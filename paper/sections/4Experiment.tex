\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:experiment}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.} We evaluate on four legal benchmarks that together
exercise distinct retrieval bottlenecks. BarExam \citep{zheng2025legalretrieval}
provides 1{,}195 bar-exam multiple-choice questions with fact patterns and
aligned legal passages. HousingQA \citep{zheng2025legalretrieval} consists of
yes/no statutory entailment questions over state housing statutes, where the
jurisdiction is recoverable from a \texttt{state} metadata field on the statute
corpus. LegalBench-SCALR \citep{guha2023legalbench} contains 571 Supreme Court
holding-selection questions with five candidate holdings. CaseHOLD
\citep{zheng2021casehold} pairs court-opinion contexts with five candidate
holdings. Two notes on scope: (i)~MuSiQue is excluded from this submission and
the legal-only scope is deliberate; (ii)~all runs use deterministic seed~42,
with calibration on rows 0--199 ($N=200$) and a held-out slice on rows 200--249
($N=50$) per dataset.

\paragraph{Models.} The four-benchmark calibration uses Gemma~4~26B-A4B served
through OpenRouter (provider key \texttt{or-gemma4-26b}). Cross-model sanity
runs use Groq Llama~3.3~70B (\texttt{groq-llama70b}). Paired comparisons hold
the model and the question subset fixed within each benchmark so that every
delta is computed over the same rows.

\paragraph{Retrieval stack.} We use a hybrid retrieval pipeline that combines
BM25 lexical scoring with dense embeddings from
\texttt{Alibaba-NLP/gte-large-en-v1.5} (1024-dimensional, 8{,}192-token
context) over per-corpus Chroma indexes. A cross-encoder reranker
(\texttt{cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2}) selects the final top-$k$ from
a candidate pool of size $3k$. BM25 is skipped for collections exceeding one
million documents, so HousingQA's statute index is dense-only. Multi-query
retrieval pools candidates across all query variants and reranks them against
the primary query.

\paragraph{Calibration and held-out protocol.} On rows 0--199 of each
benchmark we run the inherited method ladder: \method{rag\_simple} (or
\method{rag\_state\_filter} on HousingQA), \method{snap\_only\_in\_final},
\method{rag\_rewrite}, \method{rag\_hyde}, \method{rag\_snap\_hyde\_2call},
and the bottleneck-specific \controller{} routes. Per-row signals (accuracy,
gold-passage retrieval, conditional accuracy, route disagreement, parse and
error counts, and average LLM calls) are computed from the detail logs of
those runs. On rows 200--249 the controller picks routes from the calibration
signals; we report both the \emph{selected} route per row and
\emph{individual} route accuracies for ablation. Reported metrics are
multiple-choice or yes/no accuracy, McNemar $p$-values for paired
comparisons, and average LLM calls per question. Larger-$N$ evaluations
($N{\geq}500$) are in progress on all four benchmarks; we report N=200
calibration and N=50 held-out as our primary numbers and note the larger-N
expansion as future validation.

\paragraph{Statistical reporting.} All paired deltas use exact McNemar tests
with reported $b/c$ discordance counts and 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals
from 2{,}000 resamples. We do not promote single-row absolute accuracies as
primary claims; the headline number is the macro lift across the four legal
benchmarks at fixed average-call budget.
