\section{Bottleneck Taxonomy with Worked Examples}\label{app:taxonomy_examples}

Table~\ref{tab:bottleneck_taxonomy} in Section~\ref{sec:method:signals}
compresses the eight bottleneck labels onto a single page. The expanded
form below pairs each label with the cheapest calibration signal that
identifies it, the intervention the controller selects, an example trace
pattern from the actual calibration logs, and a one-sentence
interpretation. ``Trace'' figures are taken directly from the cite-clean
calibration rows in Table~\ref{tab:app_full_rows} (Gemma~4~26B-A4B,
$N{=}200$); the held-out and Llama numbers are reported as cross-checks
where relevant.

\paragraph{1. Retrieval depth.}
\emph{Signal.} A catastrophic top-1 vs.\ top-5 drop on the baseline
retrieval row: the answer accuracy collapses when only the single best
reranked passage is shown, which is the diagnostic that the gold rule is
present at rank $\leq 5$ but not at rank $1$.
\emph{Intervention.} Increase $k$ and/or use a smarter passage selector
upstream of the synthesizer.
\emph{Trace pattern.} Reference multi-hop slices show top-1 vs.\ top-5
gaps in excess of $10\pp$ on baseline retrieval; the four legal
benchmarks we run are \emph{not} depth-catastrophic, which is one half of
why the legal-RAG bottleneck taxonomy needs more than depth ablations to
be informative.
\emph{Interpretation.} The depth bottleneck is the cleanest classical
RAG failure but is empirically rare on legal multiple-choice / yes-no
benchmarks; presenting it inside the taxonomy is what allows the
controller to \emph{rule it out} as the dominant signal on the four
legal datasets and route reasoning elsewhere.

\paragraph{2. Candidate-set size then saturated.}
\emph{Signal.} Top-1 retrieval collapses (very low recall@1) yet
top-5/top-10 retrieval ties; gold retrieval rate rises but the answer
accuracy is approximately flat.
\emph{Intervention.} Restrict to a small reranked candidate set; do not
spend additional LLM calls on retrieval reformulation.
\emph{Trace pattern.} LegalBench-SCALR baseline: recall@1 $\approx 0.52$
but recall@5 $= 1.0$ and recall@10 $= 1.0$ on the same rows; the gold
rule is reliably in the top-$5$ pool but the model does not always pick
it as the single decisive holding (baseline $74.0\%$ at $N{=}200$).
\emph{Interpretation.} On SCALR, retrieval has already saturated the
useful pool; the lift has to come from \emph{candidate disambiguation},
not from looking harder for the rule. This is exactly the gap the
frontier and disagreement routes target.

\paragraph{3. Query / legal-reasoning formulation.}
\emph{Signal.} Rewrite-vs-\hyre{} asymmetry on the calibration slice;
snap-only reasoning lifts more than HyDE retrieval alone.
\emph{Intervention.} \method{rag_rewrite} or
\method{adaptive_snap_hyre_v2}; the controller selects \emph{between}
them per row.
\emph{Trace pattern.} BarExam calibration: baseline $80.0$, snap-only
$85.5$, HyDE-only $82.0$, fixed Snap-HyRE $84.5$, adaptive Snap-HyRE-v2
$86.0$. Held-out: rewrite $90.0$ vs.\ adaptive-v2 $76.0$ on the same
$N{=}50$ slice: rewrite wins this slice while the
slice-level-selected \hyre{} route ties baseline.
\emph{Interpretation.} BarExam is the benchmark on which formulation
choice (rewrite vs.\ snap-conditioned hypothetical passage) matters more
than retrieval depth. The route is \emph{which kind of pre-retrieval
text to produce}, not whether to retrieve.

\paragraph{4. Metadata / jurisdiction scope.}
\emph{Signal.} Empty-retrieval rate is high on generic top-$k$ retrieval;
an explicit metadata filter beats generic retrieval; near-zero
state-match rate without the filter.
\emph{Intervention.} \method{rag_state_filter} (lowercased Chroma
predicate over the statute \texttt{state} field).
\emph{Trace pattern.} HousingQA generic \method{rag_simple} (pre-fix)
was 0/200 empty retrieval and the filter was a parametric fallback. The
casing-repaired \method{rag_state_filter} reaches $60.5\%$ at $N{=}200$
with 81/200 gold retrieved at $k{=}5$; raw \method{rag_hyde} on the
same slice falls to $50.0\%$, and fixed Snap-HyRE to $51.5\%$ (both
negative controls).
\emph{Interpretation.} HousingQA's first-order bottleneck is
jurisdiction scope, not retrieval depth or query rewriting. Once the
state filter is in place, additional retrieval interventions degrade
the result.

\paragraph{5. Statutory entailment / false-positive yes.}
\emph{Signal.} A conservative yes/no verifier route lifts over the
state-filter baseline; fixed \hyre{} underperforms baseline. The
diagnostic is: when retrieval already finds the right statute, the model
still over-predicts yes on entailment, and a verifier prompt that
demands explicit statutory support flips the false positives.
\emph{Intervention.} \method{adaptive_snap_hyre_housing_verifier}
(state filter $+$ conservative yes/no verifier on top).
\emph{Trace pattern.} HousingQA calibration: state-filter $60.5$ $\rightarrow$ verifier
$74.5$ ($+14.0\pp$). Held-out: state-filter $62.0$ $\rightarrow$ verifier $76.0$
($+14.0\pp$). Llama~70B cross-model: state-filter $44.0$ $\rightarrow$ verifier
$60.0$ ($+16.0\pp$, transfers directionally).
\emph{Interpretation.} The HousingQA gain is the single most
transferable controller win and is what positions the verifier policy
as the strongest source-gated route in the portfolio.

\paragraph{6. Answer-option conversion.}
\emph{Signal.} Gold-retrieval gains do \emph{not} convert to answer
gains; the model has the rule in its context window but does not pick the
right one of five candidate holdings.
\emph{Intervention.} Diverse \hyre{} passages or option-table
prompting; both produce more option spread for the synthesizer to
disambiguate against. The direct option-table route is a clean negative
on this slice and is reported as a design point, not as a positive
route.
\emph{Trace pattern.} CaseHOLD: gold retrieval rises from
$16.0\%\!\rightarrow\!47.0\%$ between \method{rag_simple} and
\method{rag_snap_hyde_2call} while exact-match accuracy rises only
$69.5\%\!\rightarrow\!72.0\%$ ($+2.5\pp{}$, $b/c{=}16/11$, $p{=}0.4421$;
job 58283). At $N{=}200$ calibration the matched baseline is $73.0\%$
and diverse \hyre{} reaches $73.5\%$; held-out diverse \hyre{}
reaches $78.0\%$ vs.\ rewrite $76.0\%$ and direct option-table $70.0\%$
(job 67744).
\emph{Interpretation.} CaseHOLD's first-order bottleneck is not
retrieval but the conversion from a topical passage to a discrete
holding choice. Retrieval interventions help less than option-spread
interventions, and \emph{telling} the model the candidate holdings
without a diverse retrieval pool collapses the answer distribution.

\paragraph{7. Method disagreement / candidate exposure.}
\emph{Signal.} High row-level disagreement across the HyRE-family
rows (two routes give different predicted holdings on the same rows), and
an oracle row-level ceiling (the best-of-N across HyRE rows) that is not
realized by simple ensembles. Empirically the gold rule is in the top-$5$
pool for almost every row, so the lift is about picking among candidates,
not about candidate discovery.
\emph{Intervention.} Disagreement arbitration
(\method{adaptive_snap_hyre_disagreement_majority_prior}) or a
frontier replay selector (\method{adaptive_snap_hyre_frontier}).
\emph{Trace pattern.} LegalBench-SCALR calibration:
\method{rag_snap_hyde_2call} $76.0$, disagreement arbitrator $77.5$
($+1.5\pp{}$). Held-out: frontier $84.0$ vs.\ exact-replay route $80.0$
($+4.0\pp{}$); Llama~70B cross-model: frontier $88.0$ vs.\ baseline
$82.0$ ($+6.0\pp{}$, transfers directionally).
\emph{Interpretation.} The lift exists but the controller currently
picks the wrong route on the held-out SCALR slice. This is reported as
an open routing-policy gap, not as a closed result.

\paragraph{8. Parametric floor / depth-flat.}
\emph{Signal.} Top-1 $\approx$ top-5 $\approx$ \method{llm_only}; the
snap-only row matches RAG; the gold-conditional accuracy on the few
rows where gold is retrieved is not appreciably higher than on rows
where gold is missing.
\emph{Intervention.} Trust priors. Use snap reasoning to \emph{anchor}
the final answer, not to drive retrieval.
\emph{Trace pattern.} BarExam: $\textsf{Acc}\!\mid\!\text{gold-missing}
= 80.5\%$ vs.\ $\textsf{Acc}\!\mid\!\text{gold-retrieved} = 60.0\%$ on
baseline retrieval (the gold-conditional row \emph{inverts} on BarExam,
which is a signature of the parametric floor); BarExam llm-only is
historically $\approx 79.8\%$ on this model (see
Appendix~\ref{app:barexam_tier3}); the snap-only and snap-anchored
routes are within $\pm 2\pp{}$ of each other.
\emph{Interpretation.} BarExam reasoning is co-located with the
parametric floor: useful interventions are about \emph{anchoring} the
model's existing knowledge with a snap commit, not about feeding it more
retrieved text. The depth-flat label and the formulation label both
apply to BarExam; the controller treats formulation as the dominant
slice-level signal because rewrite-vs-\hyre{} asymmetry is the largest
cheap discriminator.

\section{BarExam Tier 3 Full-Corpus Reference Result}\label{app:barexam_tier3}

This section preserves a \emph{pre-pivot} full-corpus BarExam reference
result. The diagnostic-adaptation contribution of this paper does not
depend on it; the result is reported here because it is the closest thing
we have to a full-corpus number on any legal benchmark, and because it
supports the BarExam-route bottleneck story
(query / legal-reasoning formulation) at scale.

This is the strongest single-benchmark statistical result in the project,
and it is the cross-size robustness check for the BarExam route the
controller selects in the main paper. On the BarExam Tier 3 slice
($N{=}1{,}195$, the full BarExam test set), \method{rag_snap_hyde}
reaches $81.17\%$ vs.\ \method{rag_simple} $78.08\%$ on Gemma~4~26B-A4B
(a $+3.09\pp{}$ paired lift, $b/c{=}124/87$, McNemar $p{=}0.0130$); the
same comparison on Gemma~4~E4B is \method{rag_snap_hyde} $62.18\%$ vs.\
\method{rag_simple} $58.49\%$ ($+3.69\pp{}$, $b/c{=}172/128$,
$p{=}0.0129$). Both deltas are positive, statistically significant, and
reproduce at two model sizes on the same benchmark. The full-corpus
positive on BarExam is consistent with the calibration ladder: the
controller routes BarExam to adaptive \hyre{}-v2 precisely because
calibration shows the same lift in miniature ($86.0$ vs.\ $80.0$ at
$N{=}200$).

The full-corpus result also makes a point that the calibration and
held-out tables cannot make on their own: the BarExam route choice is not
a calibration-fitting artifact. The same intervention that wins the
$N{=}200$ slice also wins the $N{=}1{,}195$ slice, on the same model. The
limit of this evidence is that it is single-benchmark: the four-benchmark
calibration shows that fixed \hyre{} \emph{under}performs baseline on
HousingQA and CaseHOLD (negative controls in
Table~\ref{tab:app_full_rows}), so the controller's value is precisely
that it withholds \hyre{} where the negative controls predict it will
hurt and applies it where this full-corpus result predicts it will help.

The BarExam single-passage \method{golden_passage} control is the row
that most clearly motivates the depth-flat / parametric-floor reading.
Providing the labeled gold passage reaches only $78.66\%$, below both
\method{llm_only} ($79.75\%$) and \method{snap_only_in_final}
($80.59\%$). Mechanism: a labeled passage on a multi-issue bar-exam
fact pattern can be topically correct but not decisive for the question
asked, so a model that anchors on it can be \emph{pulled} away from the
right rule. Snap-first reasoning gives the model an initial legal frame
before evidence reconciliation, which is why \method{snap_only_in_final}
beats the golden-passage control on this benchmark. The general lesson
the appendix carries forward into the main paper is that
``oracle''-style controls on legal multiple-choice are diagnostic of
\emph{how the model uses} a single passage, not of a retrieval ceiling.
