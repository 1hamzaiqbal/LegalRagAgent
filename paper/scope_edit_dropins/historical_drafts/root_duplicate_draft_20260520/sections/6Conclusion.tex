\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{What the current evidence says.}
\hyre{} is best understood as a fixed generated-query intervention whose
usefulness depends on the retrieval surface of the task. It is strongest when
the raw question fails to look like the governing legal rule, as in BarExamQA.
It is less useful when the raw question already maps cleanly to source and
target legal documents, as in Legal-Link-EU. It can be positive but weaker than
plain HyDE when the task is yes/no statutory entailment, as in HousingQA. On
MASLegalBench, answer accuracy is high enough and retrieval labels are weak
enough that source-proxy retrieval should be interpreted cautiously.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The matrix is not complete. The current signoff gate has 67 signed answer
cells out of 78 expected cells, with HousingQA and Legal-Link-EU still missing
some important \hyre{} or model rows. Several signed rows carry caveats for
format retries, provider-route recovery, repair chunks, near-cap outputs, or
source-proxy retrieval. These caveats do not make the rows unusable, but they
do constrain how strongly they should be stated. MASLegalBench also lacks
official per-question gold evidence ids, so its retrieval results cannot be
compared directly to BarExamQA, HousingQA, or Legal-Link-EU Hit@5 values.

\paragraph{Historical scope.}
Older CaseHOLD and LegalBench-SCALR rows remain useful provenance for method
development, but they are not part of the active exact-scored main matrix in
this draft. This matters because the repository still contains older
controller-era text and figures. We deliberately do not revive those rows as
current main results unless the branch explicitly re-adds them and signs them
under the current method contract.

\paragraph{Next steps.}
The most valuable immediate work is not a new headline method. It is completing
the missing signed cells, especially HousingQA \hyre{} for Llama 8B,
Legal-Link-EU \hyre{} for Gemma 4 26B, and the remaining HousingQA Gemma 4
26B and rewrite rows. The tentative real-passage exemplar probe is also
promising, but it should remain a probe until it passes the same qrel, answer,
and caveat gates as the canonical method.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The main lesson is simple and sharp: legal RAG should not be evaluated as a
single answer number or a single retrieval curve. \hyre{} can convert a model's
legal priors into a useful retrieval handle, and on BarExamQA that conversion
improves final accuracy. But generated-query retrieval can also move away from
already-good raw retrieval, or fail to convert better evidence exposure into
answers. A durable legal RAG paper should therefore report the full contract:
what was retrieved, what was answered, what the oracle controls show, which
rows are missing, and which caveats travel with each claim.
