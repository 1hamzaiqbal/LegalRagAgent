\begin{abstract}
Legal retrieval-augmented generation is often reported as a single answer
accuracy number, even though the system can fail before retrieval, during
evidence selection, or after the right evidence is present. We evaluate
\hyre{}, a fixed two-call generated-query method for legal RAG. The first call
commits to a tentative answer and writes a short hypothetical legal-reference
passage; retrieval is performed with that passage; the second call answers the
original question using retrieved corpus evidence. The current source-gated
package contains 67 signed full-row answer cells out of a 78-cell exact-scored
matrix over BarExamQA, HousingQA, Legal-Link-EU, and MASLegalBench with Llama
3.1 8B, Gemma 4 26B, and Llama 3.3 70B. All result claims in this draft are
gated by \claimgate. The results are deliberately mixed. On BarExamQA,
\hyre{} improves over raw RAG for all three signed model cells, including Gemma
4 26B (78.0\% to 82.0\%) and Llama 3.3 70B (74.6\% to 79.7\%). On HousingQA,
generated retrieval helps, but question-only HyDE is stronger than \hyre{} for
Llama 70B (58.9\% vs.\ 52.4\%). On Legal-Link-EU, raw-question retrieval is
already very strong (Hit@5 0.9059), and generated-query rows generally reduce
answer accuracy. MASLegalBench is nearly saturated and has only source-document
retrieval proxies, not official qrels. The main contribution is therefore not a
universal win claim. It is a comprehensive, source-gated evaluation pattern:
report answer accuracy, retrieval exposure, oracle controls, signed caveats,
and missing cells together so that legal RAG improvements are not confused with
retrieval-only movement or provenance artifacts.
\end{abstract}
