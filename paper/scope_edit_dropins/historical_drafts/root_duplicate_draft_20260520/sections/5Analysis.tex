\subsection{Signed Answer Matrix}

Table~\ref{tab:answer_matrix} is the main result object. It supports three
high-level readings.

\paragraph{BarExamQA is the strongest \hyre{} setting.}
Raw retrieval is extremely weak on BarExamQA: the signed raw RAG retrieval
exposure is Hit@5 0.0142 and MRR@5 0.0068. Generated-query methods improve
retrieval exposure by an order of magnitude, and \hyre{} improves final answer
accuracy over raw RAG for all three signed models. The strongest signed cell is
Gemma 4 26B, where raw RAG is 932/1195 = 78.0\% and \hyre{} is 980/1195 =
82.0\%, a +4.02 pp paired improvement with McNemar $p=0.000699$. Llama 3.3
70B shows a similar signed gain: raw RAG 74.6\%, \hyre{} 79.7\%, $p =
2.70\times 10^{-5}$. This is the cleanest evidence that a snap-conditioned
legal-reference passage can be a better retrieval handle than the raw question.

\paragraph{HousingQA separates generated retrieval from best generated method.}
For Llama 70B, raw RAG reaches 47.3\%, question-only HyDE reaches 58.9\%, and
\hyre{} reaches 52.4\%. Both generated-query rows improve over raw RAG, but
HyDE is much stronger than \hyre{} (+11.57 pp over raw, compared with +5.12 pp
for \hyre{}). The signed comparison between HyDE and \hyre{} is also large:
HyDE is +6.45 pp over \hyre{} with McNemar $p=1.42\times 10^{-28}$. This
dataset is therefore not a simple ``snap helps retrieval'' example. It says
that generated legal text helps statutory retrieval and answer selection, but
the extra answer commitment in \hyre{} can be the wrong bias for yes/no
statutory entailment.

\paragraph{Legal-Link-EU is retrieval-saturated for raw RAG.}
Legal-Link-EU has very strong raw retrieval: Hit@5 0.9059 and MRR@5 0.7621
for the raw-question cache. The generated-query methods generally retrieve
less gold exposure than raw RAG and also reduce answer accuracy. For Llama 70B,
raw RAG is 75.3\%, HyDE is 72.5\%, \hyre{} is 72.1\%, and rewrite is 72.0\%.
The signed \hyre{} row is -3.19 pp below raw RAG with McNemar $p=0.0248$.
This is a useful negative result: when the raw question already expresses the
legal-link task in corpus language, a generated passage can move away from the
exact source/target document relation.

\paragraph{MASLegalBench is high-accuracy and qrel-limited.}
MASLegalBench answer accuracy is high for all models, especially Gemma 4 26B
(95.7\% LLM-only, 94.7\% raw RAG, 93.4\% \hyre{}). Retrieval exposure is a
same-source-document proxy rather than an official gold-passage qrel. The raw
same-source proxy is higher than the generated-query proxies, but answer
accuracy is already near saturation. The paper therefore treats MAS as an
answer robustness and source-proxy retrieval setting, not as a primary
gold-passage retrieval benchmark.

The signed \hyre{} deltas make the paper's central caution visible. A method
can be real and useful without being universally useful. \hyre{} helps in the
BarExamQA setting that motivated the method, but generated legal passages are
not automatically better retrieval handles for tasks whose raw question already
names the right documents or whose answer space is nearly saturated.

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval_matrix} and Figures~\ref{fig:retrieval_answer_delta}
and~\ref{fig:retrieval_by_method} are the mechanism layer. BarExamQA sits in
the desirable quadrant: generated queries increase Hit@5 and final accuracy.
Legal-Link-EU is the opposite: generated queries lower Hit@5 relative to a very
strong raw retriever and lower answer accuracy. HousingQA shows that two
generated-query methods can both improve over raw RAG while one of them is
clearly stronger. MASLegalBench shows why proxy retrieval has to be labeled:
same-source exposure can move in a different direction from already-high final
accuracy.

\subsection{Oracle Evidence Controls}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{26_oracle_gap.png}
  \caption{Oracle evidence controls relative to raw RAG. Gold passage rows
  show answer-synthesis headroom; gold-plus-neighbor rows test whether adding
  nearby legal text helps or distracts. MASLegalBench has no official
  gold-passage rows and is omitted.}
  \label{fig:oracle_gap}
\end{figure*}

Oracle rows are not ceilings in a pure sense, but they expose how much answer
headroom remains if retrieval is bypassed. HousingQA has a large oracle gap:
for Llama 70B, raw RAG is 47.3\%, gold passage is 67.3\%, and gold plus
neighbors is 66.0\%. Legal-Link-EU also has large oracle gains, especially for
Gemma 4 26B (raw RAG 78.5\%, gold passage 96.0\%). BarExamQA is different:
oracle rows are only slightly above or sometimes below generated-query rows,
which suggests that answer selection and option reasoning can dominate the
value of a single gold passage. Across datasets, gold-plus-neighbor rows are
often below gold-only rows, showing that extra plausible legal text can dilute
rather than improve a gold signal.

The caveat ledger is not bookkeeping trivia; it is part of the empirical
claim. Many citable rows are clean in the sense of zero errors, no missing
predictions, and no fallback markers, but still require a caveat for
same-model answer-format retries, near-cap original outputs, repair chunks,
source-proxy retrieval, or same-model provider-route recovery. A legal RAG
paper that hides these caveats would overstate the cleanliness of the matrix.

\subsection{Takeaway}

The signed matrix supports a narrow, defensible conclusion. \hyre{} is a useful
fixed generated-query method for BarExamQA and some other cells, but it is not
a universal replacement for raw RAG or HyDE. The value of the paper is the
joint accounting: answer accuracy, retrieval exposure, oracle controls, cost,
coverage, and caveats move together. That joint view is what turns mixed
results into a methodologically useful result rather than a failed leaderboard
story.
