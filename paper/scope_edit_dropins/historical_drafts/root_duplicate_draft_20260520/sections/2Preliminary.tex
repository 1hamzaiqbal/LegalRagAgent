\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:preliminary}

\paragraph{Retrieval-augmented generation.}
RAG couples a parametric generator with a nonparametric evidence store
\citep{lewis2020rag}. The usual promise is modular: a retriever supplies
fresh or domain-specific evidence, and the generator conditions on that
evidence to answer. This promise is especially plausible in law because many
answers turn on identifiable text. But legal RAG inherits the same failure
interfaces as open-domain RAG: the retrieval query may not match the corpus
language, the right passage may be ranked below distractors, the generator may
ignore or misuse evidence, and the evaluation may conflate these stages.
Fusion-in-decoder and related architectures \citep{izacard2021fid} emphasize
how much downstream answer quality depends on the reader's ability to use
retrieved passages, not just on whether a retriever can surface them.

\paragraph{Generated-query retrieval and HyDE.}
HyDE generates a hypothetical document from a query and embeds that document
for dense retrieval \citep{gao2023hyde}. Query2doc and related expansion
methods similarly ask a language model to turn sparse query text into richer
retrieval handles \citep{wang2023query2doc}. These methods are attractive for
legal questions because the question stem may describe facts while the corpus
uses doctrinal or statutory language. \hyre{} follows this generated-query
line but adds a snap answer before the generated passage. The passage is meant
to look like the legal rule that would resolve the snap, not like a final
answer explanation. That distinction is small but important: the first call
can use the model's legal priors to choose a retrieval vocabulary, while the
second call still answers from retrieved corpus evidence.

\paragraph{Adaptive and agentic RAG.}
Several systems decide when to retrieve or how to revise generation at
inference time. Self-RAG learns reflection tokens for retrieval and critique
\citep{asai2024selfrag}; Adaptive-RAG routes by question complexity
\citep{jeong2024adaptive}; active and corrective variants add retrieval or
repair steps when uncertainty or criticism is triggered
\citep{jiang2023flare,yan2024crag}. The current paper is intentionally not a
controller paper. Earlier drafts in this repository explored diagnostic
routing, but the current branch evaluates a fixed method. We still borrow the
evaluation discipline from adaptive RAG: report not only whether an answer is
right, but also which stage appears to be binding.

\paragraph{Legal NLP and legal RAG.}
LegalBench, CaseHOLD, LexGLUE, and Pile of Law helped standardize legal
language-understanding and legal-reasoning evaluation
\citep{guha2023legalbench,zheng2021casehold,chalkidis2022lexglue,henderson2022pileoflaw}.
Recent work has also highlighted that legal assistants can hallucinate or cite
unreliably in practice \citep{magesh2024hallucinations}, motivating source
grounding and careful audit trails. Legal RAG benchmarks such as LegalBench-RAG
and Legal RAG Bench emphasize the need to evaluate retrieval and answer quality
together \citep{pipitone2024legalbenchrag,butler2026legalragbench}. Our matrix
uses two related legal QA tasks, BarExamQA and HousingQA, plus two project-local
exact-scored legal retrieval-answer tasks, Legal-Link-EU and MASLegalBench.
Legal-Link-EU provides source/target document golds for a legal-link prediction
task over a frozen EU-law corpus. MASLegalBench provides multiple-choice
penalty-notice questions over a frozen source corpus, but no official
per-question passage qrels; we therefore report its retrieval metrics as
same-source-document proxies.

\paragraph{Evaluation stance.}
The paper uses a strict source-gated stance. We distinguish four kinds of
numbers. Final answer accuracy is the downstream task metric. Hit@5/MRR@5 are
retrieval exposure metrics only when gold evidence ids align with the active
corpus. Oracle controls inject the gold passage, or the gold passage plus
neighbors, and are diagnostics rather than deployable systems. Finally,
operational coverage describes which expected cells are signed, active, or
missing. This separation prevents a common legal RAG mistake: treating a better
retrieval curve as if it were already a better answer system.
