\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:preliminary}

\paragraph{Retrieval-augmented generation.}
RAG couples a parametric generator with a nonparametric evidence store
\citep{lewis2020rag}. The usual promise is modular: a retriever supplies
fresh or domain-specific evidence, and the generator conditions on that
evidence to answer. This promise is especially plausible in law because many
answers turn on identifiable text. But legal RAG inherits the same bottlenecks
as open-domain RAG: the retrieval query may not match the corpus
language, the right passage may be ranked below distractors, the generator may
ignore or misuse evidence, and the evaluation may conflate these stages.
Fusion-in-decoder and related architectures \citep{izacard2021fid} emphasize
how much downstream answer quality depends on the reader's ability to use
retrieved passages, not just on whether a retriever can surface them.

\paragraph{Generated-query retrieval and HyDE.}
\hll{Please **briefly** introduce HyDE in the Introduction section. You can introduce more details about HyDE here, if needed.}
HyDE generates a hypothetical document from a query and embeds that document
for dense retrieval \citep{gao2023hyde}. Query2doc and related expansion
methods similarly ask a language model to turn sparse query text into richer
retrieval handles \citep{wang2023query2doc}. These methods fit
legal questions because the question stem may describe facts while the corpus
uses doctrinal or statutory language. \hyre{} follows this generated-query
line but adds a preliminary answer before the generated passage. The passage
should look like the legal rule or statutory condition implicated by that
candidate answer signal, not like a final answer explanation. The risk is that
answer-conditioning can also import a wrong prior or erase useful anchors; the
evaluation therefore treats \hyre{} as a hypothesis about retrieval handles, not
as guaranteed self-verification. For example, a yes/no housing question may describe a
tenant's conduct, while the retrieval corpus phrases the relevant law as a
landlord's statutory remedy. In theory, the first
call can use the model's legal priors to choose a retrieval vocabulary, while
the second call still answers from retrieved corpus evidence.

\paragraph{Fixed-method stance.}
Some retrieval-augmented systems decide when to retrieve or how to revise
generation at inference time, for example by learning reflection tokens or
adding corrective retrieval steps \citep{asai2024selfrag,jiang2023flare,yan2024crag}.
Here, every benchmark cell uses the same fixed method ladder\hll{I don't quite get the meaning of "ladder". I notice this notion is also used in the Method section. You can explain it a little bit, or use another expression.}, and the analysis
asks where the fixed \hyre{} intervention helps or hurts. The borrowed
evaluation discipline is
stage separation: report not only whether an answer is right, but also which
retrieval, oracle, or formatting stage appears to be binding.

\paragraph{Legal NLP and legal RAG.}
\hll{legal RAG is enough. No need to mention legal NLP.} Legal NLP benchmark suites and legal-text corpora have helped standardize
language-understanding, legal-reasoning, and corpus-grounded evaluation
\citep{guha2023legalbench,chalkidis2022lexglue,henderson2022pileoflaw}.
Recent work has also highlighted that legal assistants can hallucinate or cite
unreliably in practice \citep{magesh2024hallucinations}, motivating source
grounding and careful audit trails. Legal RAG benchmarks such as LegalBench-RAG
and Legal RAG Bench emphasize the need to evaluate retrieval and answer quality
together \citep{pipitone2024legalbenchrag,butler2026legalragbench}. Our matrix
uses two related legal QA tasks, BarExamQA and HousingQA, plus two project-local
exact-scored legal retrieval-answer tasks, Legal-Link-EU and MASLegalBench.
Legal-Link-EU provides source/target document golds for a legal-link prediction
task over a frozen EU-law corpus. MASLegalBench provides multiple-choice
penalty-notice questions over a frozen source corpus, but no official
per-question passage labels; we therefore report its retrieval metrics as
same-source-document proxies.

\paragraph{Evaluation stance.}
\hll{Please move this part to experiment section} We report only result rows whose answer, retrieval, and metric records are
complete enough to interpret. We distinguish four kinds of numbers. Final
answer accuracy is the downstream task metric. Hit@5/MRR@5 are retrieval
exposure metrics only when benchmark relevance labels identify which passages
or documents count as correct evidence: Hit@5 asks whether such evidence
appears in the top five, while MRR@5 gives more credit when it appears earlier.
Oracle controls inject the gold passage, or the gold passage plus
neighbors, and are diagnostics rather than deployable systems. Finally,
coverage describes which expected cells are complete, open, or not applicable.
We include both downstream performance and retrieval performance, as within a real world system both are relevant.

\subsection{Snap-HyRE Mechanics}\label{sec:method:snaphyre}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{21_snap_hyre_method_flow.png}
  \caption{\hyre{} flow. The first model call produces both a preliminary
  answer and a retrieval-shaped hypothetical legal reference passage. Retrieval
  uses the passage. The second model call receives the retrieved evidence and
  the original question; the preliminary answer is recorded but not handed in as
  evidence.}
  \label{fig:method_flow}
\end{figure*}

For a formatted legal question $q$, \hyre{} performs:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Preliminary answer and passage generation.} The model receives the dataset's
direct-answer prompt plus instructions to emit a required final answer line and
a separate \texttt{\#\# Passage} block. The preliminary answer lets model
priors select legal vocabulary for retrieval. The passage must be short,
neutral, and reference-style: it should describe the controlling rule,
statutory condition, legal relationship, or doctrinal test without using answer
labels or an \texttt{Answer:} header.
\item \textbf{Output validation.} The first call must contain both a preliminary answer
in the dataset's answer format and a separate retrieval passage. Outputs that
do not satisfy this interface, that include option labels such as
\texttt{Answer: (C)} inside the retrieval passage, or
that produce overlong passages are excluded from reported results.
\item \textbf{Retrieval.} The retriever uses only the hypothetical
legal-reference passage as the query. The preliminary answer is not evidence.
Retrieval uses the same dataset corpus, embedding model, hybrid retrieval
stack, and cross-encoder reranking path as the other retrieval rows.
\item \textbf{Final answer.} The final model call receives the retrieved
evidence and the original formatted question. It must emit the dataset-specific
final answer line, such as \texttt{Answer: (C)} or \texttt{Answer: Yes}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\hyre{} for one question}
\label{alg:snaphyre}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Format question $q$ using the dataset answer interface.\hll{Consider adding some pseudo code. The current content doesn't look like an algorithm.}
\STATE Generate preliminary answer $a_0$ and passage $p$ from $q$.
\STATE Validate that $a_0$ has the required answer format and $p$ is a clean retrieval passage.
\STATE Retrieve and rerank top-$k$ evidence using $p$ as the only query.
\STATE Answer the original question from retrieved evidence; do not supply $a_0$ as evidence.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{LLM only.}
\method{llm_only} uses the final formatted question and no retrieved evidence.
It is the parametric answer baseline.

\paragraph{Raw RAG.}
\method{rag_simple} performs no query-generation call. It retrieves with the
raw retrieval text for the dataset: BarExamQA includes the shared fact pattern
when present, HousingQA uses the question text under the state filter for main
rows, and Legal-Link-EU/MASLegalBench use the question text that carries the
benchmark's document or source anchors. The final answer call receives the
retrieved top-$k$ evidence and the fully formatted question.

\paragraph{HyDE.}
\method{rag_hyde} generates a hypothetical legal reference passage from the
retrieval-side question representation in Table~\ref{tab:dataset_interface}.
For multiple-choice datasets, this representation may include unlabeled
candidate-answer framing, but the HyDE passage is instructed not to choose an
option or emit an answer line. The row isolates whether generated legal prose
helps retrieval independent of the snap answer prior used by \hyre{}.

\paragraph{Rewrite.}
\method{rag_rewrite} asks a query-rewriter prompt to emit structured search
queries from the same retrieval-side question representation used by HyDE and
\hyre{}. The rewrite call emits JSON with a primary query and optional
alternative queries. Retrieval pools candidates from the emitted query strings,
deduplicates document ids, and applies the same reranking and top-$k$ truncation
as the other retrieval rows. Invalid rewrite outputs are excluded rather than
silently replaced with raw-question retrieval.

\paragraph{Oracle controls.}
\method{golden_passage} injects the labeled gold passage. \method{golden_plus_neighbors}
injects the labeled gold passage as Source 1, then retrieves with the gold
passage text as the neighbor query, removes the gold id from the neighbor pool,
and fills the remaining $k-1$ context slots using the same corpus, metadata
filter, deduplication, and reranking path as the dataset's retrieval rows. These
rows are especially important in law because a single labeled passage may be
under-specified, while additional legal neighbors may either supply needed
context or introduce distracting authority. MASLegalBench has no official
passage-level gold labels, so these oracle rows are not defined there.

\paragraph{Probe-only exemplar variants.}
We also track a non-canonical probe called \method{snap_hyre_exemplar}. It keeps
the same two-call \hyre{} structure but adds one fixed, dataset-specific
real-passage-style exemplar to the first-call generation prompt. The exemplar
has its associated question, answer, and document id removed. It is therefore a
style signal about what a useful corpus passage looks like, not row-specific
answer evidence. The companion \method{rag_hyde_exemplar} control applies the
same style signal without the preliminary answer. These rows are not part of the
audited main matrix unless they later pass the same generation, retrieval,
answer, and audit gates as canonical \hyre{}.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

Retrieval exposure is evaluated at Hit@1/5/10 when passage-level gold evidence
is available. The main answer matrix uses the same final evidence depth,
$k=5$, for all non-oracle retrieval rows.

Unless a row-level note says otherwise, runs use seed 42 for dataset sampling
and cache naming, temperature 0 for hosted model calls, and strict
no-silent-fallback execution. The retrieval stack uses Chroma collections built
with \method{Alibaba-NLP/gte-large-en-v1.5} embeddings, optional BM25 candidate
pooling when feasible for the collection, and
\method{cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2} reranking before top-$k$
truncation. Answer rows use a 2048-token completion cap with logged
same-model/same-evidence format retries for malformed final answer lines.

Rows are included in paper-facing claims only when they satisfy the following
conditions:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The method, model, dataset, and answer depth match the intended row.
\item Final answers are present and parseable in the dataset-specific format.
\item Each evaluated example has one retrieval record, enough retrieved documents
for the requested $k$, and benchmark-provided relevance labels when available.
\item The result record names the metric values and any paper-facing note needed
to interpret the row.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Metrics}

The downstream metric is exact answer accuracy. Multiple-choice tasks use the
last valid answer line, such as \texttt{Answer: (A)} through
\texttt{Answer: (D)} or \texttt{Answer: (E)} depending on the dataset. HousingQA uses
\texttt{Answer: Yes} or \texttt{Answer: No}. Retrieval exposure is Hit@5 and
MRR@5 where the dataset supplies benchmark relevance labels that identify the
correct passages or documents. HousingQA also tracks
multiple-gold statute recall internally, but the main table reports any-gold
Hit@5/MRR@5. MASLegalBench has no official per-question gold passage ids, so
retrieval exposure is reported as a same-source-document proxy.

We also report paired McNemar tests when available, but we do not
require every audited row to have a significance comparison. Many of the most
important findings are directional comparisons across retrieval and oracle
diagnostics rather than a single pairwise test.

\subsection{Audited Answer Matrix}

\input{tables/current_answer_matrix.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:answer_matrix} is the main results table. It supports four
high-level readings.

\paragraph{BarExamQA is the strongest \hyre{} setting.}
Raw retrieval is extremely weak on BarExamQA: the audited raw RAG retrieval
exposure is Hit@5 0.0142 and MRR@5 0.0068. Generated-query methods improve
retrieval exposure by an order of magnitude, and \hyre{} improves final answer
accuracy over raw RAG for all three audited models. It also beats HyDE
retrieval on all three models, which is the most direct evidence that the snap
answer can sharpen the generated legal passage beyond a question-only
hypothetical answer. The strongest audited cell is Gemma 4 26B, where raw RAG
is 932/1195 = 78.0\% and \hyre{} is 980/1195 = 82.0\%, a +4.02 pp paired
improvement with McNemar $p=0.000699$. Llama 3.3 70B shows a similar audited
gain: raw RAG 74.6\%, \hyre{} 79.7\%, $p = 2.70\times 10^{-5}$. This is the
cleanest evidence for the proposed mechanism in a weak-raw-retrieval setting.

\paragraph{HousingQA is a jurisdiction-interface result.}
The signed HousingQA Llama retrieval rows in this draft are retained as
unfiltered provenance rather than final main-matrix claims. In those rows,
generated retrieval changes answer accuracy substantially: for Llama 70B, raw
RAG is 47.3\%, HyDE is 58.9\%, \hyre{} is 52.4\%, and rewrite is 55.8\%; for
Llama 8B, \hyre{} is essentially tied with raw RAG (57.4\% versus 57.3\%),
while rewrite reaches 63.4\%. The fair main-matrix interpretation, however, is
that HousingQA needs a state-aware retrieval interface because the unfiltered
national corpus can retrieve plausible wrong-jurisdiction statutes. The
state-filtered raw retrieval check raises raw Hit@5/MRR@5 from the old
unfiltered 0.0282/0.0148 to 0.3695/0.2330. The state-filtered Llama 70B
\hyre{} retrieval check is lower, 0.2311/0.1427. Two state-filtered answer
rows are marked with daggers rather than cited as answer-accuracy results.
HousingQA therefore tests whether \hyre{} adds value after jurisdiction scope
is fixed; the unfiltered rows do not settle the method ranking.

\paragraph{Legal-Link-EU is retrieval-saturated for raw RAG.}
Legal-Link-EU has very strong raw retrieval: Hit@5 0.9059 and MRR@5 0.7621
for the raw-question row. The generated-query methods generally retrieve less
gold exposure than raw RAG and often reduce answer accuracy. The boundary is
not uniform, though. For Gemma 4 26B, \hyre{} retrieves more gold exposure than
HyDE (Hit@5 0.6788 versus 0.4898) and rewrite (0.5439), and improves final
accuracy over HyDE (78.3\% versus 74.2\%) while remaining at practical parity
with raw RAG (78.3\% versus 78.5\%). For Llama 70B, \hyre{} also retrieves
more gold exposure than HyDE and rewrite (Hit@5 0.7249 versus 0.5466 and
0.6584), but final accuracy trails raw RAG. This is a useful boundary result,
not a harness failure: when the raw question already expresses the legal-link
task in corpus language, a generated passage can move away from exact
source/target document anchors.

\paragraph{MASLegalBench is high-accuracy and proxy-labeled.}
MASLegalBench answer accuracy is high for all models, especially Gemma 4 26B
(95.7\% LLM-only, 94.7\% raw RAG, 93.4\% \hyre{}). Retrieval exposure is a
same-source-document proxy rather than a passage-level gold-evidence metric.
The raw same-source proxy is higher than the generated-query proxies, but
answer accuracy is already near saturation: \hyre{} improves over raw for
Llama 8B (88.1\% versus 86.1\%), is within the $\pm$1 pp parity band for
Llama 70B (92.1\% versus 92.7\%), and is a modest miss for Gemma 4 26B
(93.4\% versus 94.7\%). The paper therefore treats MAS as an answer
consistency and source-proxy retrieval setting, not as a primary gold-passage
retrieval benchmark.

\subsection{Oracle Evidence Controls}

Oracle rows are not ceilings in a pure sense, but they expose how much answer
headroom remains if retrieval is bypassed. These controls test use of a labeled
passage under the paper's prompt and context contract; they are not upper bounds
on task accuracy, especially when a single gold passage omits
option-disambiguating context. The unfiltered HousingQA provenance rows have a
large oracle gap: for Llama 70B, raw RAG is 47.3\%, gold passage is 67.3\%,
and gold plus neighbors is 66.0\%. This is not a state-filtered HousingQA
answer claim. Legal-Link-EU also has large oracle gains, especially for Gemma
4 26B (raw RAG 78.5\%, gold passage 96.0\%).
BarExamQA is different: oracle rows are only slightly above or sometimes below
generated-query rows, which suggests that answer selection and option reasoning
can dominate the value of a single gold passage. Across datasets,
gold-plus-neighbor rows are often below gold-only rows, showing that extra
plausible legal text can dilute rather than improve a gold signal.

The full oracle-control table is in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:additional}.

\subsection{Takeaway}
\hll{You can add small text boxes after each subsection findings.}
The audited matrix supports a positive but bounded conclusion. \hyre{} is a
solid fixed generated-query method in the regime it was designed for: legal
questions whose raw wording is a poor retrieval query. It does not need to be a
universal replacement for raw RAG to be useful, because the boundary cases are
interpretable rather than arbitrary. The value of the paper is the joint
accounting: answer accuracy, retrieval exposure, oracle controls, coverage,
worked examples, and qualification notes move together. That joint view turns
mixed results into a methodologically useful result rather than a
leaderboard-only story.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
\hll{Let conclusion be a separate section. The limitations can be put in another separate section, because the limitations don't count in the 8-page limitation.}
\paragraph{What the evidence says.}
\hyre{} is best understood as a fixed generated-query intervention whose
usefulness depends on the retrieval surface of the task. It is strongest when
the raw question fails to look like the governing legal rule, as in BarExamQA.
It is less useful when the raw question already maps cleanly to source and
target legal documents, as in Legal-Link-EU. It can be positive but weaker than
plain HyDE in the unfiltered HousingQA provenance rows; those rows are not
state-filtered main-matrix claims. On MASLegalBench, answer accuracy is high
enough and retrieval labels
are weak enough that source-proxy retrieval should be interpreted cautiously.
For a page-limited workshop version, this argues for a sharper main/appendix
split: use BarExamQA and the state-filtered HousingQA interface as primary
method tests, and use Legal-Link-EU and MASLegalBench as boundary analyses
showing when raw anchors or source-proxy labels dominate.

\paragraph{Evaluation limitations.}
The matrix is partially signed. It has 61 signed main-matrix cells out of 78
expected cells under the HousingQA state-filter rule. Ten unfiltered HousingQA
retrieval-context cells are kept as starred provenance, two state-filtered
HousingQA answer cells are marked with daggers and excluded from signed answer
claims, and the HousingQA Gemma 4 26B LLM-only row is omitted because it lacks
a complete signed result. Several completed rows carry paper-facing qualification notes
for format validation, output length, endpoint identity, segmented evaluation,
or source-document proxy retrieval. These notes do not make the rows unusable,
but they do constrain how strongly they should be stated. MASLegalBench also
lacks official per-question gold evidence ids, so its retrieval results cannot
be compared directly to BarExamQA, HousingQA, or Legal-Link-EU Hit@5 values.

\paragraph{Method limitations.}
\hyre{} can erase useful anchors. Legal-Link-EU and MASLegalBench show that a
generated legal passage may paraphrase away source documents, relation strings,
or source-notice cues that the raw question already exposes. HousingQA shows a
different boundary: if retrieval is not jurisdiction-filtered, generated legal
language may still retrieve plausible law from the wrong state. Finally,
retrieval exposure is not answer conversion; even oracle evidence can be
diluted by neighboring legal text or under-used by the answer model.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The natural next evaluation step is a signed state-filtered HousingQA answer
matrix, followed by a small set of worked examples where the generated \hyre{}
passage explains a retrieval or answer improvement over raw RAG and other
generated-query controls. The real-passage exemplar probe is hypothesis
generating, especially as an anchor-recovery analysis on Legal-Link-EU and
MASLegalBench, but it should remain a probe unless it passes the same
retrieval, answer, and inclusion criteria as the canonical method.
The main/appendix boundary follows the same logic: BarExamQA and
state-filtered HousingQA test the weak-query setting most directly, while the
source-proxy and anchor-rich analyses explain why \hyre{} is not always best.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The main lesson is simple and sharp: legal RAG should not be evaluated as a
single answer number or a single retrieval curve. \hyre{} uses model priors to
form a neutral legal retrieval target, then forces the final answer to use
retrieved evidence. On BarExamQA, that separation improves final accuracy. But
generated-query retrieval can also move away from already-good raw retrieval,
or fail to convert better evidence exposure into answers. A credible legal RAG
evaluation should therefore report what was retrieved, what was answered, what
the oracle controls show, which rows are missing, and which qualification notes
apply.

The retrieval-passage exemplar probe note describes a probe-only
\method{snap_hyre_exemplar} variant. It adds one fixed, sanitized
